# Staged Hero scene endpoint cache

This internal optimization applies only inside the existing opt-in staged-navigation path, after the Hero destination has bound and while its interaction inputs are held. Dock minimaps, missing High, animated assets, unsupported materials and invalidated scenes retain live rendering. It does not change the independent background-reveal p and eased Low/High-refinement q clocks. The supporting pixel experiments and their limitations are recorded in [the isolated proof](scene-endpoint-pixel-proof.md).

`SpatialElementRefinement` owns the existing Low/High capture targets. After the last warm live pass, it draws High then Low at p=0, copies those resolved colors into exactly two additional RGBA16F targets, and draws High then Low at p=1 into the original targets. The added targets have no depth, stencil or MSAA; together they are at most 8 MiB under the existing 512×1024-pixel cap. A cached frame mixes these four linear scene endpoints, then applies the same premultiplied overlay material/blend path as the live refinement. The normal full-resolution High draw at refinement completion remains unchanged, and releases the extra endpoint colors.

Taking endpoints and hidden warm draws do not advance q, its frame count or completion state. Before warming, the held List destination may adopt already-ready asynchronous High, matching the Carousel's prior binding flush; it never forces loading/decoding or waits for optional High. The Stage keeps a narrow hidden-preparation clock hold through the GPU fence. An intervening regular RAF may read the submitted endpoints in GPU queue order while that hold is active; it cannot mark them ready or tick q. Only a fence ticket may mark them ready.

The cache composite's shader warmup uses the real output attachment with a zero-area **RenderTarget** scissor and `autoClear=false`. It restores those settings even on failure and must not modify the destination seed. This matters for transmission: opacity below one makes a hidden warm overlay observable in the next real frame. The actual-API GPU proof compares destination pixels before/after preparation exactly and does not reset the candidate seed afterwards.

Every live Low/High target write has a separate write epoch. A ticket records that epoch, the actual output-target identity/size, and the scene guard. A live write during the fence, including one with an unchanged page generation, prevents that ticket from installing stale endpoints. A hidden pending read is explicitly distinguished from a write and tested. There is no automatic rewarm/retry loop: invalidation releases the extra colors and resumes live rendering.

`sceneEndpointEligibility` snapshots the scene, both representations, camera, additional background/tint scenes and their cameras, static lights, geometry attributes, materials, textures and known environments. Reveal validation compares those recorded values without traversing or cloning the scene. It accepts a narrow standard-material set and rejects custom/Node shaders, unsupported blending, dynamic textures, skinning/morphing/instancing and animated assets. Material/geometry/texture disposal invalidates the guard. The controller separately checks asset animation clips, because GLTF clips need not live on `Object3D.animations`.

The outer owner also checks page-binding generation, held Hero identity, active scene target/quality/size, background color, media mode and cancellation. Finish, abort, supersede, resize/refit, resource mutation, owner transfer, replacement and dispose release or invalidate the endpoint lease. Stale ticket cancellation cannot release a newer lease. Additional guard listeners are disposed with the lease.

The existing query-gated stage diagnostics expose `getCatalogPresentation().representations.sceneEndpointCache`. Per-refinement counters distinguish preparations, ready installations, ready hits, pending reads, invalidations and stale tickets. Browser acceptance must prove actual hits for both List and Carousel Hero navigation; merely seeing fewer GPU passes is insufficient. Unsupported/private material variants may correctly stay live and must not be reported as cache hits.

`playwright.scene-endpoint-navigation.config.ts` runs two separate functional cases and samples those counters during the actual revealing phase. It explicitly waits for available High so that eligibility is testable; it is not the performance harness and cannot establish latency acceptance. The existing 22-case navigation suite and performance cohort readiness rules remain unchanged.

CPU tests cover state-neutral captures, bounded attachments, independent p/q, pending reads versus a real fence overwrite, output resize before any RAF, cancellation, replacement, guard/resource failure, ownership transfer, output-seed preservation during shader warmup, ready-only High adoption and the unchanged final High draw.

Build95956/49c5bc7-wip passed both corrected-runtime v2 and actual-cache-API v3 GPU proofs (48 samples each, unchanged tolerances), plus the two actual navigation hit cases. List recorded ready1/hits20/pendingReads4 during reveal; Carousel ready1/hits11/pendingReads0. Both ended in High. See the [pixel proof report](scene-endpoint-pixel-proof.md) for immutable artifact labels, intermediate failed controls and numerical bounds. The full functional matrix and performance acceptance remain separate; these functional probes do not establish smoother or faster navigation.

Eligibility is intentionally conservative. A follow-up guard review identified nonphysical negative/invalid authored radiometric values and unusual Float/HalfFloat material/vertex data as further boundaries: a shader's final nonnegative clamp can break affine mixing. Those variants must stay live unless separately validated; the passing standard-material fixtures do not prove arbitrary numeric inputs safe.
